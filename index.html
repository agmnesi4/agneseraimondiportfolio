01About me

My background in Fashion and Costume Design (BA) gave me a hands-on understanding of materials, textiles, and the human body, along with the craft skills to turn ideas into physical form. Costume design also taught me how objects and materiality tell stories and shape the way we see ourselves and others. I later expanded this foundation with a Master’s in Design for Emergent Futures, where I learned to read cultural shifts, research emerging trends, and build narratives about the relationships between people, materials, and society, while also exploring new technologies.

At the core of my practice is biomaterials and biofabrication with living systems. I work with them to explore alternatives to conventional ways of designing, building, and making, and as a starting point for questions about sustainability, regeneration, and co-creating with nature. I’m also passionate about ecovillages and permaculture, and I spend time in these communities whenever I can, bringing insights from different fields back into my research.

Alongside my practice, I design and facilitate workshops on biomaterials and natural dyes to encourage experimentation and open conversation.

02Methodology / process

I work with biomaterials, which are materials that originate from living matter such as plants, algae, fungi or bacteria rather than from petrochemicals. I use different kind of bioplastics, biocomposite and bioceramic made from natural ingredients and natural dyes extracted from plants. And the biofabrication, an approach to making in which biological processes and organic matter are not raw resources to be forced into a predetermined shape, but active participants whose behaviour has to be understood, accommodated and, to some extent, trusted. For example, I use the biofabrication of roots, to create a textile (see the section Work for further explaination).

That lack of complete control is precisely what interests me. A bioplastic shifts as it dries, a dye reacts differently to each fibre and each material, it can change colour with a shift in the pH, and sometimes you simply can’t control external factor that will influence the growth of the material. Of course certains biomaterials can be reproduced with a reliable recipe, yet I love to work with the concept of transformation and co-creation, and I find that this is not a limit of the technique but the very source of each piece’s uniqueness.

Working with these materials and with what the land offers also imposes a different rhythm and a different approach, that moves from exploitation to collaboration. It is also the starting point of my workshops and collaborations, which tend to open onto wider conversations about how we relate to matter.

03Work

Education

Master in Design for Emergent FuturesBarcelona, 2025 – 2026

A hands-on, multidisciplinary master where I worked daily in the biolab, learned digital fabrication and researched Pleasure Activism.

+ Read more– Close

I did my Master in Design for Emergent Futures in Barcelona, organised by IAAC and ELISAVA Barcelona School of Design and Engineering, in collaboration with Fab Lab Barcelona and the Fab Academy. It is a hands-on, multidisciplinary programme that takes design beyond aesthetics, to face complex challenges where many different factors are connected. The programme is built around four pillars: Explore, Build, Reflect and Apply. Through the Fab Academy, based on MIT’s course “How to Make (Almost) Anything” and led by Neil Gershenfeld, we learned digital fabrication, coding and synthetic biology, and we looked critically at technologies such as AI, blockchain and biotechnology.

What I loved most is that I had the chance to work every day in the biolab, a laboratory where you research and experiment with living materials, and to deepen my research on biomaterials. I also explored new technologies: I learned some coding and how to work with Arduino, a small programmable board that lets objects react to the world around them, and I discovered new software such as Rhinoceros, TouchDesigner, and learned how to 3D print. I integrated all of this into my projects.

My research project was about Pleasure Activism and what it means for women to live our own intimacy and pleasure, starting from my experience. I developed group facilitations to start conversations on the topic, and I prototyped games and objects of exploration to go with them. And working side by side with my classmates, I also learned rapid prototyping: turning ideas into real objects quickly, testing them and improving them together.

Selected projects

(UN)chastity

2025

(UN)chastity is a pair of pressure-sensitive g-string underwear made from biomaterials, embedded with vibration motors that respond to touch pressure. We explored and experimented at the intersection of e-textiles, biodesign, and intimate wearable technology, trying to make our artefacts as much open source and DIY as we could.

If you want to know more, the project is fully open source and you can find it on Hackster.
